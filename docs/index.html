01 · Philosophy — risk-first sizing

Most order tickets ask "how much size / what leverage?" Viridis inverts that. You state the one thing you actually know — how many dollars you're willing to lose — plus your entry and stop. Everything else is derived:

The whole idea risk ($) + entry + stop  ⟶  position size, leverage, and a full OTOCO bracket
  • Position size comes from risk, never from margin or a leverage slider.
  • Leverage is computed so liquidation always sits behind your stop — it is a capital-efficiency knob, not a risk knob.
  • "Risk" means net loss — the size is solved so that the price move to your stop plus round-trip fees equals the dollars you typed.

The result: if you're wrong you lose exactly what you said; if you're right you keep the rest. Leverage changing from 20× to 50× does not change either number — only how much margin is locked.

02 · Architecture & signal flow

Sharply separated modules. All exchange I/O is non-blocking; all GUI updates travel through Qt signals.

cache_engine.py
Instrument symbology + every risk-limit tier per symbol (MMR, max leverage, cap). Paginated pull, cached to disk (24h TTL), O(1) lookup.
trading_core.py
The engine: risk math, OTOCO execution, REST + dual WebSocket state machine, Strat1 ratchet, risk ledger, journal reconciliation. No GUI. The single writer of trade state.

Append-only trade journal, deduped by closing order ID, mirrored from Bybit's closed-PnL. Stats, windows, streaks, R / MFE / MAE.

Palette + stylesheet; painted primitives (ladder, mini ladder, R histogram, equity curve, hinted input, meter); composite views (trade card, journal dialog) and text formatting.

03 · The risk math

Position size (fee-aware)

When a long is stopped out you lose the price move and pay fees on the notional — a maker fee on the limit entry and a taker fee on the market stop. Both scale with notional, which balloons as the stop tightens. So the size is solved with the fees inside the denominator:

Fee-aware quantity qty = risk  /  ( |entry − sl|  +  entry·fmaker  +  sl·ftaker ) 
# Worked example — BTC long, entry 74,300 · SL 73,300 · risk $50
fee/unit  = 74300·0.0002 + 73300·0.00055   = 55.18
qty       = 50 / (1000 + 55.18)            = 0.04738  → 0.047 (round down to step)

# Naive sizing (risk/|entry−sl|) would give 0.050 and a TRUE loss of $52.76 — a 5.5%
# overshoot here, ballooning to ~37% on a 0.2% stop. Fee-aware holds the loss at $50.

Safe leverage

Leverage is chosen so the liquidation distance always exceeds the stop distance, with a cushion for the stop order's own fill slippage. The exact isolated-margin liquidation distance for a long is:

Liquidation distance (fraction of entry) liq_dist = ( 1/L − MMR ) / ( 1 − MMR )

The engine uses the slightly conservative approximation 1/L − MMR (dropping the 1−MMR denominator makes it under-estimate the distance — i.e. it errs toward safer, lower leverage), and requires it to clear the stop plus a buffer:

Chosen leverage L = floor( 1 / ( sl_distance + MMR + fee_cushion ) )   capped at the symbol's max 
# BTC: sl_distance = 1000/74300 = 1.346% · MMR = 0.5% · cushion = 0.2%
L = floor( 1 / (0.01346 + 0.005 + 0.002) ) = floor(48.9) = 48×
liq ≈ 74300 × (1 − 1/48 + 0.005) = 73,124  → 0.23% behind the 73,300 stop

Which MMR? The tier the notional lands in

Bybit raises the maintenance margin rate — and lowers max leverage — in risk-limit tiers as position value grows. Half of all linear symbols end tier 1 at $5k or less, and a $100 risk on a 0.5% stop is ~$20k of notional. Sizing with the base-tier MMR on such a symbol puts the real liquidation inside the stop. The cache therefore stores every tier, and calculate_trade picks the MMR and max leverage for the notional it just computed. Once the position is live, the exchange's own liqPrice is tracked and compared to the stop; if it ever sits inside, the log shouts.

What the cushion is for

The stop and the liquidation are both evaluated on mark price, so the stop always triggers first; volatility on the way down cannot reorder them. The cushion (LIQ_CUSHION_PCT, 0.2% by default, plus whatever the integer leverage rounding adds) is the room for the stop's market order to fill before mark can travel on to the liquidation price. It only fails if a single mark step exceeds it during those milliseconds — a flash crash on a major, a cascade on a thin alt. Widening it costs margin and nothing else: on the BTC example 0.5% means 45× instead of 53×, about $12 more locked for the same $50 at risk.

Why a Mark-Price stop trigger?

Liquidation is always evaluated on Mark Price. If your stop triggered on Last Price, your cushion would be measured between two different rulers — and Last vs Mark diverge exactly during the violent moves where the cushion matters. Triggering the SL on Mark puts it on the same ruler as the liquidation engine, so the stop is guaranteed to fire first. The leverage cushion above is then simply the room for the market stop to fill. Configurable via SL_TRIGGER_BY.

04 · Order execution — the atomic bracket

A single place_order call carries the entry plus its dormant TP/SL, so the entry and its bracket arrive together (OTOCO). With tpslMode="Partial" the TP is a real limit order (maker fee), the SL is a market order (guaranteed exit), and multi-target scale-outs stay possible later.

{
  orderType:   "Limit",   price: entry,   qty: <fee-aware>,
  takeProfit:  <midpoint trigger>,  tpOrderType: "Limit",  tpLimitPrice: tp,
  stopLoss:    sl,           slOrderType: "Market",  slTriggerBy: "MarkPrice",
  tpslMode:    "Partial",   timeInForce: "GTC" (or "PostOnly" — cancel instead of crossing the book)
}

The ticket shows the symbol's live last / bid / ask and spread while you type (click the price to use it as entry) and warns when a limit entry would cross the book — a marketable limit fills immediately as taker, at a different price and fee than the sizing assumed. post-only is on by default and turns that warning into an exchange-side guarantee. After the fill, the private execution stream verifies the assumption: a taker entry is flagged in the log with actual vs planned fees. A live position can always be exited instantly via close mkt (reduce-only market order + a sweep that cancels any orphaned TP/SL conditionals).

The midpoint-trigger trick (for a maker TP fill)

The TP trigger is set at the midpoint between entry and target, while the limit price sits at the target. So when price reaches the midpoint, a limit order is placed at the target and rests on the book — by the time price arrives, it fills as a maker, not a taker.

SLmarket exit
entrylimit fill
midpointTP trigger fires → limit rests
TPmaker fill
Long shown. The limit sell is placed at the target the moment price crosses the midpoint, so it's resting (maker) before price gets there.

05 · Concurrency model

This is the most intricate part of the system: four execution contexts cooperate, and exactly one of them is allowed to touch the GUI. Understanding the lanes is the key to understanding the code.

① Qt main thread
  • All widget rendering & reads
  • 500ms panel-flush timer
  • Spawns workers for execute / modify / cancel / close and the 30s REST reconciliation, so it never blocks and never touches the exchange itself
② Private WS
  • order_stream → fills, cancels, TP/SL hits
  • position_stream → uPnL, mark, realised PnL
  • execution_stream → per-fill fees, maker/taker truth
  • wallet_stream → live equity
  • Mutates TradeState under the lock
③ Public WS
  • ticker_stream → live mark + last/bid/ask cache
  • Computes live PnL locally
  • Feeds the pre-trade price display
  • Evaluates Strat1 each tick
④ Worker queue + REST reconciliation
  • Runs REST ops dispatched from the WS threads (Strat1 amends, ledger writes)
  • Every 30s: account, positions + brackets, journal — the core updates its own state and emits snapshots; a fill younger than 15s is trusted over the REST snapshot
shared state: self._trades (dict of TradeState)  ·  guarded by a single RLock  ·  every cross-thread update reaches the GUI only as a clone() snapshot over a Qt signal

Two patterns make this safe: (1) snapshots, not shared objects — threads emit clone() copies so the UI never reads an object mid-mutation; (2) optimistic local update + rollback — Strat1 updates its in-memory stop immediately to suppress duplicate triggers, dispatches the REST amend to the worker, and rolls back if the amend fails.

06 · Trade lifecycle

Each trade is a small state machine keyed first by our client order ID, then re-keyed to Bybit's order ID once it's known.

A partial fill that gets its remainder cancelled promotes straight to LIVE at the filled size. A LIVE trade can also exit via close mkt (reduce-only market order, recorded as a Manual close). On boot, sync_existing() rebuilds this machine for any positions or resting orders already on the account — including ones placed elsewhere — and re-arms Strat1 from the risk ledger on trades that were opened with it. The status pill reflects actual WebSocket health (polled every 5s) and degrades visibly to the 30s REST fallback when a stream drops.

07 · Strat1 — the de-risking ratchet

Strat1 is a deliberately simple heuristic, not a profit-maximiser. Because targets sit near expected resistance, once price has "basically done the job" the goal is to not give it back — so the stop ratchets as price progresses along the entry → TP journey. The table is configurable (STRAT1_RATCHET, "progress:lockR, …"); the default is the original two-step trail:

TriggerActionPhase
≥ 75% to TPLock −0.5R: stop → entry ± (half the original stop distance)1
≥ 90% to TPLock 0R: stop → true break-even (offset past entry to cover round-trip fees, so a stop-out nets ~$0)2

A positive lock (say 0.9:0.5) sits at break-even plus that many R. The phase is the number of steps applied, so a fast move skips straight to the highest step reached. Two guards: the stop never loosens (a manually tightened stop beyond the ratchet level just records the phase) and never crosses the mark (a lock past the current price would be a market exit — the step is skipped with a loud log line). Amendments are rate-limited to one per 5 seconds per symbol, applied optimistically in memory, executed on the worker thread, and rolled back if the exchange rejects them. Whether it is worth it is a question for the journal: every app-placed trade records its MFE and MAE in R, so the giveback Strat1 saves can be measured against the winners it cuts short.

Strat1 survives restarts. The original SL/TP and the current phase are persisted in the risk ledger at placement and on every successful amend; on boot, sync_existing() matches the position back to its ledger entry (by client order ID for resting entries, by symbol + entry price for live positions) and re-arms the trail at the recorded phase. Trades placed before this persistence existed can't be restored — the app logs a loud warning instead of failing silently.

10 · The board

The window is a ticket, a set of position cards, and an account-level risk strip. The rule behind all three: every number the trade depends on is on screen before the trade exists, and every action on a live trade is one click on the card it belongs to.

Ticket

  • Live preview. Every keystroke recomputes leverage, quantity, notional, margin, fees, R:R, liquidation and cushion. Warnings are ranked — amber asks for a confirm, red blocks — and the confirm sheet lists them again before anything is sent.
  • Relative inputs. Stop as a price or a distance (-0.8%), target as a price, a percent (+3%) or an R multiple (2R), risk in dollars or as a share of equity (1%). The hint inside each field shows the form you did not type. Arrow keys nudge by tick (shift ×10), Enter moves on, ⌘Enter executes, Esc resets.
  • Ladder. Target, mark, entry, stop and liquidation on one price scale, each with its distance, the dollars at that level and the R. The cushion between liquidation and stop is visible, not implied.

Cards

One per active trade. At rest a card is a header — symbol, side, entry → mark, PnL in $ and R — over a mini ladder with stop, target and liquidation. Clicking a card unfolds its details (leverage, quantity, the cushion behind the stop, progress to target, MFE/MAE in R, Strat1 phase, a taker-entry flag) and its actions — stop to break-even, stop to −0.5R, close at market, cancel, edit TP/SL; only one card is unfolded at a time, and hovering any card shows the same numbers as a tooltip. Actions resolve to the core's own trade object, so the next ticker snapshot carries the change instead of reverting it. A stop that would trigger immediately is refused before it is sent.

Governance strip

Open risk (sum of dollars at the stops across active trades, derived from the bracket for positions the app did not place), today's realised PnL against the daily limit, and the loss streak. The caps are .env settings; a breached daily limit disables execute until tomorrow. This is what replaced the exposure heat bar: notional over equity says nothing under leverage-derived sizing, dollars at the stops do.
